What Is Mindfulness? A Plain Guide (and How It Overlaps With Qigong)

By Lin Hai · MBSR & mindfulness trainee (100+ hrs) · Updated October 7, 2026 · 5 min read

Mindfulness gets packaged as a wellness trend, but the core idea is ordinary and useful: pay attention, on purpose, in the present moment, without judgment. That definition, from Jon Kabat-Zinn, is the one clinicians use. Here's the plain version, and where it meets the Chinese practice of qigong.

Mindfulness vs meditation

People mix these up. Meditation is a practice you sit down to do. Mindfulness is the quality you train during that practice and then carry into the day — noticing you're rushing, or tense, and choosing to respond rather than react. One is the gym; the other is the fitness.

The science (MBSR)

Mindfulness entered clinics through MBSR (Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction), created by Jon Kabat-Zinn at UMass in 1979. Studies link regular practice to lower perceived stress, better attention, reduced reactivity, and higher heart-rate variability (HRV) — a marker that the nervous system is recovering and calm. This is the same mechanism qigong research points to.

Where mindfulness meets qigong

The two overlap more than people expect. Both train attention, breath, and body awareness. Qigong adds slow movement and an "energy" (qi) vocabulary; mindfulness is usually still and described in psychology terms. A qigong standing practice is a mindfulness practice with your eyes open and your feet rooted.

How to start

  • Sit or stand comfortably. Set a timer for 5 minutes.
  • Feel your breath at the lower belly. Let it slow naturally.
  • When the mind wanders (it will), notice it and return to the breath. That return is the training.
  • End by noticing your hands — the same "feel your qi" doorway from the What Is Qi? video.

Consistency beats length. Five minutes daily beats an hour once a month.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is mindfulness in simple terms?

Mindfulness is paying attention, on purpose, in the present moment, without judgment. It is a trainable quality of awareness, not a belief.

What is the difference between mindfulness and meditation?

Meditation is a practice you sit down to do. Mindfulness is the aware, non-judging quality you train during that practice and then carry into daily life.

Is there science behind mindfulness?

Yes. Research links mindfulness training to lower stress, better attention, reduced reactivity, and higher heart-rate variability (a marker of a calmer nervous system).

How is mindfulness different from qigong?

They overlap: both train attention, breath, and body awareness. Qigong adds slow movement and an energy (qi) framework; mindfulness is usually still and described in psychology terms.

How do I start mindfulness?

Start small: five minutes of slow breathing, noticing the breath and gently returning attention when the mind wanders. Consistency matters more than length.
