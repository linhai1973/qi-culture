Moving to New Zealand with a Family: A Chinese Journalist's Honest Field Notes

By Lin Hai · 25-year media professional · Updated October 7, 2026 · 6 min read

These are personal field notes from my own relocation journey, not immigration or legal advice. Visa rules change — always confirm current details with Immigration New Zealand.

Most "move abroad" content is either a sales pitch or a horror story. After 25 years in Chinese media and a family relocation in progress, here are the honest, unglamorous notes — the things I wished someone had told me plainly.

Why New Zealand

For a family, the pull is simple: clean environment, low crime, and a public education system where a child can attend a state school without tuition fees on the right visa. It is also a place where a calmer, outdoor, slower life is genuinely possible — not a slogan.

The visa pathways that matter for a family

  • Student + family: a student visa can come with a partner open work visa and free state schooling for the dependent child.
  • Green List / skilled residence: for roles on the Green List (for example, certain ICT and specialist roles), residence may be reachable more directly, which can also secure the child's schooling and the partner's work rights.

The right path depends on your occupation, age, and English. Get the current rules from the official source — not from a blog.

Schools

The widely shared point is true: a child is generally entitled to free domestic tuition at a state school while the family holds the appropriate visa. The fine print — enrolment zones, uniform costs, stationery, and the occasional donation request — is where real budgets live. Visit schools in person before committing to an area.

Cost of living, honestly

It is higher than many newcomers expect, especially housing and winter heating. Auckland and Wellington cost more than regional towns. The trade is often a lower-stress life and less time lost to congestion — but that is a values call, not a universal win.

What surprised me

  • The outdoor culture is not a brochure; weekends genuinely revolve around beaches, trails, and water.
  • The pace — slower, and at first frustrating if you're used to a newsroom.
  • Everyday multiculturalism — immigrant stories are normal, not exceptional.

The hard parts

Distance from aging parents. Credential and experience recognition. Building a professional network from zero in your 50s. None of these is a reason not to go; they are reasons to plan, not to romanticize.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can my child go to school for free in New Zealand?

Generally yes, at a state school, while the family holds a visa that entitles the child to domestic tuition (for example, as a dependent of a student or work visa holder). Always confirm the current rules with Immigration New Zealand, as policies change.

Can my spouse work in New Zealand?

Often yes. A student visa holder's partner may be eligible for an open work visa, and some skilled residence pathways let the partner work. Eligibility depends on the specific visa and the principal applicant's course or role.

Is New Zealand expensive to live in?

Cost of living is real and often higher than newcomers expect, especially housing and heating. It varies by city; smaller towns are cheaper than Auckland or Wellington.

Is New Zealand safe and good for raising kids?

By global standards, New Zealand is safe with strong public education and outdoor culture, which many families value. "Good for kids" still depends on your own needs, community, and proximity to support.

How long does the visa process take?

Processing times vary by visa type and demand and change over time. Check the official Immigration New Zealand website for current estimates rather than relying on older figures.
